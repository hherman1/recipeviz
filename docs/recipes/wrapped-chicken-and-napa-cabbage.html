Wrapped Chicken and Napa Cabbage

Five-spice marinated chicken legs sealed in foil packets over napa cabbage, which steams in everything the chicken gives off.

Serves 4, in 2 packets · 45-50 minutes, plus 4-12 hours marinating

Reverse-engineered from a wrapped chicken eaten in Shenzhen.

Ingredients

Meat

  • 1.4 kg chicken, as 4 legs (Sakura chicken in the original)

Marinade

  • 11 g fine salt
  • 0.5 g white pepper
  • 1 g five-spice powder
  • 2 g sugar
  • 8 g oyster sauce
  • 8 g light soy sauce
  • 40 g fresh ginger, thin slices
  • 20 g garlic, 6 cloves, lightly smashed

Vegetable

  • 800 g napa cabbage

Topping

  • Chili oil

Notes

The marinade is written by weight, and it is worth keeping it that way. 11 g of salt on 1.4 kg of chicken is a hair under 0.8% — a seasoning ratio, not a brine. Over 4-12 hours that is enough to season the meat through without curing it into something firm. Scale the salt with the chicken rather than the marinade's other numbers.

The packet is a steamer, not a roasting tray. Nothing in this recipe browns. The chicken renders, the cabbage collapses into the rendered fat and the marinade, and the foil keeps all of it in contact for the full 45 minutes. A packet that leaks turns into dry roast chicken on wilted cabbage.

Cabbage underneath, chicken on top. In a sealed packet this is the whole mechanism — everything the chicken gives off has one place to go. It is the reason 800 g of a fairly plain vegetable ends up tasting like the best part of the dish.

75°C is above the safe point for chicken, and deliberately so. Legs are dark meat with plenty of connective tissue; they are better a few degrees past done than pulled early, and there is no crisp skin here to protect by rushing.

The marinating window is the only thing to plan around. Everything after it is assembly and an oven timer.
